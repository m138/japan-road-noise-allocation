Nighttime road traffic noise and near-road NO2 in Japan

Population-weighted means of 12.3 million receptors in grid cells of about 0.4 km

Map by municipality

Point to a cell

Move the pointer over the map, or tap it, to see the values of a cell.

Nighttime noise. Outdoor Lnight (22:00–06:00) from the roads of the 2021 Road Traffic Census, at receptors 4 m high that were placed at random within each census small area in proportion to its population (about 10 residents per receptor). The model considers geometric spreading only. Screening by buildings and noise barriers is not included, and roads outside the census are not counted. The floor of the model is 40 dB, which applies to receptors more than 200 m from a census road. A cell shows the population-weighted energy mean of its receptors.

Near-road NO2. The part of the annual mean NO2 of fiscal year 2022 that the national model assigns to nearby roads. A cell shows the population-weighted mean of its receptors.

Reading the map. Cells are 0.004° (about 0.4 km), the same as in the enlarged maps of Fig. 1 of the paper. Coarser cells are shown at small scales. Cells without residents are not colored, and gray cells have few residents. Outside cities a cell often holds only one or two receptors, so the value of a single cell is uncertain. The map shows the pattern of exposure. It is not an assessment of individual addresses or buildings.

Source: D. Matsushita, “Sleep benefits and neighborhood disparities in the allocation of road traffic noise mitigation in Japan” (submitted). Code: github.com/m138/japan-road-noise-allocation (MIT). Background map: GSI Tiles (Geospatial Information Authority of Japan).
